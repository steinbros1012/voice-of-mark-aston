Symptoms & Situations

What Kind of Emergency Are You Dealing With?

Not sure whether what you’re experiencing counts as an emergency? Find your situation below. If you’re in doubt, call us — we’d rather hear from you early.

  • Broken or Chipped Tooth

    A cracked or chipped tooth can expose the sensitive layers underneath and get worse quickly. Even a small chip is worth having looked at.

    Rinse gently with warm water and save any pieces of the tooth in a clean container to bring with you. A cold compress on the outside of your cheek can help with swelling. Call us so we can see how deep the damage goes.

  • Loose Tooth

    An adult tooth that moves is never normal. It can point to an injury, an infection, or a problem with the bone and gum supporting the tooth.

    Try not to wiggle or test the tooth, and avoid biting on that side. Stick to soft foods and call us right away — a loose tooth can often be stabilised when it's treated quickly.

  • Broken or Lost Crown or Filling

    When a crown or filling comes out, the tooth underneath is left unprotected and is often sensitive to air, heat, and cold.

    Keep the crown if you still have it and bring it with you. Avoid chewing on that side and keep the area clean. Please don't try to re-cement it yourself with household glue — call us and we'll re-secure it properly.

  • Toothache

    Persistent tooth pain usually means something needs attention — often decay, an infection, or a crack that isn't visible from the outside.

    Rinse with warm salt water and floss gently to clear anything trapped between the teeth. A cold compress can ease swelling. Use over-the-counter pain relief only as directed on the label, and don't place aspirin directly against the gum. Call us so we can find the cause rather than just the symptom.

  • Object Lodged Between Teeth

    Food or debris wedged between teeth can press on the gum and become painful or infected if it isn't removed.

    Try gentle flossing first. Never use a pin, knife, or other sharp object to dig it out — that can damage the gum or the tooth. If it won't come free, call us and we'll remove it safely.

More dental emergencies we treat

  • Swelling in the Mouth

    Swelling in the gums, face, or jaw can be a sign of infection and should be evaluated promptly.

    Apply a cold compress to the outside of your face and call us the same day.

    If the swelling is spreading toward your eye or neck, or you have any trouble breathing or swallowing, go to the emergency room or call 911 first.

  • Broken Braces

    A broken bracket or a wire that has come loose can poke and cut the inside of your mouth.

    Cover the sharp end with orthodontic wax or a small piece of clean gauze to protect your cheek. Please don't cut the wire. Contact your orthodontist — and call us if you're bleeding or in pain.

  • Bit Tongue or Cheek

    Bites to the tongue, lip, or cheek can bleed heavily and are sometimes more serious than they first appear.

    Clean the area gently, then press with clean gauze to control the bleeding. A cold compress helps with swelling.

    If bleeding doesn't stop after about 15 minutes of steady pressure, seek emergency medical care.

  • Injured Jaw

    A blow to the jaw can cause a fracture or dislocation, and it needs to be evaluated promptly.

    Support the jaw with a cloth or wrap and apply a cold compress. Do not force your jaw open or closed.

    For serious trauma — or if you can't close your mouth, are bleeding heavily, or may have hit your head — go to the emergency room.

  • Impacted or Inflamed Wisdom Teeth

    Wisdom teeth that are impacted or infected can cause pain, swelling, and difficulty opening the mouth fully.

    Rinse with warm salt water and use a cold compress for comfort. Call us for an evaluation — Dr. Long has training in oral surgery and can talk you through the right next step.
